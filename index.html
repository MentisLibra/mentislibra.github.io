This Week · May 5–10

Record highs on strong earnings — Iran ceasefire holds, but oil stays above $95

The S&P 500 closed Friday at a new all-time high of 7 399, capping a sixth consecutive weekly gain (+2.3%) — the longest winning streak since 2024. The Nasdaq surged +4.5% on the week, driven by blowout earnings from Alphabet (+34% YTD) and AMD (+57% data center revenue YoY).

The April NFP jobs report surprised to the upside at +115 000 — well above the 60 000 consensus — with unemployment steady at 4.3%. Healthcare and transport drove the beat. The data confirmed the labour market remains resilient despite war uncertainty and elevated energy costs.

The dominant geopolitical theme remains the US-Iran conflict. The fragile ceasefire announced April 7 held through the week despite fresh skirmishes in the Strait of Hormuz. An Axios report mid-week of a near-final peace memorandum sent oil down 7% to $95 before it partially rebounded. WTI remains up +65% year-to-date. The Strait stays largely closed.

Gold traded near $4 731, down sharply from its February peak of $5 275 — a $540 drop driven by the Iran conflict freezing the Fed's rate-cut path. With March CPI at +3.3% YoY (energy +10.9%), the market now prices zero Fed cuts in 2026. New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh is awaiting Senate confirmation. Consumer sentiment hit a new low at 48.2.

Ahead · May 12–16

CPI Tuesday, PPI Wednesday, Warsh vote — the most critical inflation week of the year

Next week is the most important macro week since the war began. April CPI (Tue) and PPI (Wed) arrive back-to-back and will form the Fed's last complete inflation picture before the June 16–17 FOMC — the first meeting that may be chaired by Kevin Warsh.

April CPI (Tuesday May 12, 8:30 ET) is the centrepiece. March printed +3.3% YoY driven by a 21.2% gasoline surge. A softer April print — if energy costs retreated before the latest Hormuz escalation — could revive rate-cut hopes and push equities further. A hot print would confirm the market's zero-cut scenario and weigh on bonds.

PPI (Wednesday May 13) rounds out the picture. Watch energy and transport components — any divergence from CPI will signal whether consumer-level relief is sustainable or front-loaded.

The Warsh confirmation vote is expected this week. His communication style will differ markedly from Powell — less transparency, fewer forward guidance signals. Markets will need to reprice how they read the Fed.

On the geopolitical front, Iran's response to the US peace proposal via Pakistan is the wildcard. Any renewed escalation would send oil above $110 and reset the entire macro picture.

Le rapport NFP d'avril a surpris à la hausse avec +115 000 créations d'emplois, bien au-dessus du consensus de 60 000. Le chômage est resté stable à 4,3%. Santé et transport ont tiré le chiffre. Le marché du travail reste résilient malgré l'incertitude géopolitique.

L'or s'est traité près de $4 731, loin de son pic de février à $5 275. Le CPI de mars est ressorti à +3,3% sur un an (+10,9% pour l'énergie). Le marché price désormais zéro baisse Fed en 2026. PPI (mercredi 13 mai) complète le tableau. Les composantes énergie et transport seront scrutées — toute divergence avec le CPI signalera si le répit consommateur est durable.

Le vote de confirmation de Warsh au Sénat est attendu cette semaine. Son style de communication — moins de transparence, moins de forward guidance — obligera les marchés à recalibrer leur lecture de la Fed.

Sur le plan géopolitique, la réponse iranienne à la proposition de paix US via le Pakistan reste le joker de la semaine.
